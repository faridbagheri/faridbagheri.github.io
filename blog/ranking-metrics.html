MRR, MAP, and Precision@K in plain language

Evaluation · Information Retrieval · Recommendation

When a system returns a ranked list of reviewers, one metric cannot describe every aspect of quality. I often think about three complementary questions.

MRR: how early is the first good result?

Mean Reciprocal Rank rewards systems that place the first relevant reviewer near the top. If the first relevant reviewer appears at rank 1, the reciprocal rank is 1. If it appears at rank 2, it is 1/2.

MAP: how good is the full ranked list?

Mean Average Precision considers the positions of multiple relevant reviewers. It is useful when there can be several correct reviewers and we care about their ordering across the result list.

Precision@K: how clean is the shortlist?

Precision at K asks how many of the first K recommendations are relevant. For example, Precision@3 focuses directly on the quality of the top-three shortlist.

Use them together

MRR emphasizes the first relevant result, MAP captures broader ranking quality, and Precision@K measures the usefulness of the top portion of the list. Together they give a more balanced picture of recommendation performance.
