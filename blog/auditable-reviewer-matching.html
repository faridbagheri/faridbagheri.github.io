What makes reviewer matching auditable?

Research notes · Reviewer recommendation · LLMs + Knowledge Graphs

In reviewer recommendation, a strong score is useful, but it is not enough. An assignment becomes easier to trust when we can inspect why a reviewer was ranked highly.

Expose the evidence

A practical way to improve auditability is to keep intermediate evidence visible: extracted keywords, semantic concepts, graph relations, and the similarity components that contribute to a final score.

Separate representation from ranking

The pipeline can treat representation and ranking as separate steps. Text is first transformed into semantic features; then paper and reviewer profiles are compared using explicit similarity functions such as cosine similarity and Jaccard similarity.

Evaluate ranking quality directly

Metrics such as MRR, MAP, and Precision@K help answer different questions about the ranked list. This makes system evaluation more informative than relying on one aggregate score.

Why structured knowledge helps

Knowledge Graphs provide an additional representation layer that can capture relationships among entities and concepts. Combined with LLM-derived signals, they can support matching that is both semantically rich and more inspectable.
